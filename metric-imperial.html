          Cup-to-gram conversions use typical weights for recognised ingredients (flour, sugar, butter, milk and others). Anything unrecognised is converted by volume.

          Good to know when following recipes

          • A US cup is 237 ml. Australian, NZ and Canadian recipes often use a 250 ml metric cup. Old UK recipes use ounces by weight.
          • A US tablespoon is 14.8 ml, a metric one 15 ml, and an Australian one 20 ml. For most recipes the difference doesn't matter.
          • "Ounce" can mean weight (28 g) or fluid volume (30 ml). Check whether the recipe says fl oz.
          • Flour weight per cup varies by up to 15% depending on how you scoop it. Weighing in grams is the most reliable.
          • A US stick of butter is ½ cup, 8 tbsp or 113 g.
          • Fan ovens run hotter. Lower a conventional temperature by about 20 °C (25 °F).

              Good to know

              • These are the European Central Bank's reference rates, published once each working day around 16:00 CET. They are mid-market rates with no fees.
              • Banks, cards and exchange booths add a margin, typically 1–3% for cards and 5–10% at airport kiosks.

              Trousers and jeans

              Jeans are often sized as waist × leg length in inches, such as 32×32. To convert, multiply inches by 2.54: a 32 in waist is 81 cm, and a 34 in leg is 86 cm. The Distance & length tab converts any other value.
